Why this exists

The casino bid is over. The site still needs a plan.

When Sands first proposed an integrated resort at the Nassau Veterans Memorial Coliseum, it asked for community input. Nabet answered with a plan meant to keep what a resort wanted and add something both fans and neighbors could recognize: a home for the Jets on the old Mitchel Field grounds.

Sands announced in April 2025 that it would not pursue a New York gaming license. It still holds a long lease and has said it would look for another developer. That is the opening. This proposal is the backup plan Nabet has been carrying since 2023, updated so it does not pretend the casino application is still alive.

He also spoke at the Town of Hempstead board in May 2025 as founder of the civic association. The original write-up lives at sayyestothejets.org.

The proposal

Eight pieces. One Hub.

Same bones as Joe’s plan, written so a legislator can skim it and a neighbor can share it.

01

Stadium

Retractable or fixed roof for the Jets and possibly the Cosmos. Dual grass fields stored below grade. Sportsbook lounge. About 76,000 seats.

02

Resort hotel

Four- and five-star hotel in the spirit of the old Sands ask: dining, spa, meetings, and a rooftop public deck. Casino only if a license actually exists.

03

Veterans park

Flags, fountain, eternal flame, and 1972 dedication plaques from the Coliseum, set in a Hempstead Plains garden.

04

Tailgate towers

Two open-air garages built for tailgating, with vents and filtration so smoke does not sit in the structure.

05

Tailgate village

West-side yard with a kitchen, bar, screens, and room for bands. Open to the public on non-event days.

06

Pedestrian bridge

A safe crossing of Hempstead Turnpike so the Hub is one place, not two parking lots.

07

LIRR station

New stop on Endo Boulevard using an upgraded Garden City–Mitchel Field secondary, plus satellite parking for event exit.

08

Farmingdale HQ

Optional practice site on unused land by Republic Airport, 8.7 miles east, if the Jets ever want to leave Florham Park.

Stadium

Keep the Coliseum’s name in the building.

Nabet’s line is that scraping the arena and forgetting it would waste a veterans memorial. The concept keeps plaques, photos, and branch seats — Army, Navy, Marines, Air Force, Coast Guard, Space Force, POW/MIA, first responders, and 9/11 — inside a new bowl.

The roof is the practical part. Nassau weather kills outdoor dates. A retractable or fixed roof, with two field trays in an underground greenhouse, lets the building take football, soccer, concerts, and expos without painting the grass every week. Bernabéu is the reference, not MetLife.

1
Event days

Football or soccer field rolls up. South end stays open to the park.

2
Show days

Fields drop. Floor becomes concert, expo, or family-show space.

3
Every other day

Tours, a Jets hall, a Coliseum museum, and the park stay open.

What neighbors actually get

A park that is not a parking lot with flags.

The Nassau Veterans Memorial Park is the public half of the deal: flag circle from the old arena, memorial fountain, eternal flame, and a botanical walk planted for the Hempstead Plains. It is the answer to “what is here on a Tuesday.”

Traffic is the real objection, and the plan treats it as a design problem. On-site garages are not enough for 76,000 people leaving at once. The ask is a Mitchel Field LIRR stop, bus rapid transit already studied by the county, a Hempstead Turnpike bridge, and satellite lots with shuttles.

    Straight answers

    Questions people actually ask.

    Is this official?

    No. Joseph Nabet runs it as a civic association. The Jets have not announced a move from MetLife. Nassau County has not adopted this plan.

    Did Sands approve it?

    No. Sands asked for community input, then dropped the casino-license bid in April 2025. This site no longer treats a Sands casino as a done deal. A hotel can stand without a gaming license.

    Who pays for a stadium?

    The proposal does not invent a financing sheet. Any real version needs the team, a developer on the lease, and a public vote or legislative deal on the county-owned land. Signing this petition does not commit tax dollars.

    What about traffic?

    The Hub is already car-bound. The plan adds a rail stop, a turnpike bridge, filtered tailgate garages, and off-site lots. Without those, the stadium piece should not be built.

    What changed from the old site?

    Typos cleaned up. The Sands timeline is current. The “bomb the decision makers” line is gone. The petition exports a real list. The page says, in plain type, that this is not the Jets.
